AEO vs GEO vs SEO: What a B2B SaaS Founder Actually Needs to Know

AEO, GEO and SEO explained for B2B SaaS founders: one-line definitions, a comparison table, 2026 data on AI search, and which to prioritise at your stage.

By Anupam Varshney, Founder, Octaloop

Short answer: SEO gets your pages ranked in search results. AEO (answer engine optimisation) gets your brand named and cited inside AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, and GEO (generative engine optimisation) is mostly another name for the same work. A B2B SaaS founder needs SEO as the foundation and AEO on top of it, because buyers now build shortlists inside AI chats.

The labels confuse people because the industry hasn't agreed on them. This guide gives you clean definitions, shows where the three overlap and where they don't, and tells you which one to work on first at your stage.

What is SEO?

Search engine optimisation (SEO) is the work of getting your pages to rank in traditional search results (Google, Bing) so buyers click through to your site. The levers are familiar: content that matches search intent, technical crawlability, internal links and backlinks from trusted sites. Success is measured in rankings, organic clicks and the pipeline those clicks create.

What is AEO?

Answer engine optimisation (AEO) is the work of getting your brand mentioned, recommended and cited inside the answers that AI tools generate. The "answer engines" are ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, and Google's own AI Overviews and AI Mode. Success is measured by how often you're named for the buyer questions that matter, whether you're linked, and whether you're described correctly.

What is GEO?

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is a term formalised in a KDD 2024 research paper on making content more visible in generative engine responses (Aggarwal et al.). In practice, most marketers use GEO and AEO for the same goal. HubSpot calls the difference "mostly terminological" and uses AEO as the umbrella term (HubSpot). EMARKETER notes there's no common taxonomy yet, with GEO, AEO, GSO, LLMO and AIO all used for overlapping tactics (EMARKETER). The measurement body AMEC frames GEO slightly more broadly, covering how an organisation is represented in AI answers, not only whether it appears (AMEC).

Our take: don't spend a meeting on the label. If an agency pitches GEO and another pitches AEO, ask both the same question: which buyer prompts will you get us named in, on which engines, and how will you prove it?

Why can't a SaaS founder rely on SEO alone anymore?

Three sets of 2026 numbers explain it.

1. Your buyers start in AI chats. In a March 2026 survey of 1,076 B2B software buyers, G2 found that 51% now start their software research in an AI chatbot more often than in Google, 71% rely on AI chatbots somewhere in their research, and 69% said a chatbot led them to choose a different vendor than they'd planned (G2, April 2026; press release).

2. The audience is huge. OpenAI said at DevDay on 29 September 2026 that ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users (OpenAI). Google said in May 2026 that AI Mode has passed a billion monthly active users globally (Google).

3. Ranking first earns fewer clicks than it used to. Ahrefs found that when an AI Overview appears, the top-ranking page's click-through rate is about 58% lower, based on December 2025 Search Console data for 300,000 keywords (Ahrefs, Feb 2026). Seer Interactive's data across 53 brands shows that pages cited in an AI Overview get more than twice the CTR of uncited pages on the same results page (about 2.1% vs 0.9%) (Search Engine Land, April 2026). Being cited is now part of winning the click.

Doesn't ranking on Google get you into AI answers anyway?

Only partly, and less than many founders assume.

  • Google AI Overviews: In Ahrefs' 2026 analysis of 863,000 results pages, about 38% of pages cited in AI Overviews also ranked in the top 10 for the same query, down from about 76% in its earlier study. Ahrefs suggests AI Overviews now lean more on the related sub-searches Google runs behind the scenes, which it calls query fan-out (Ahrefs, March 2026).
  • Other AI assistants: Across 15,000 prompts, Ahrefs found only about 12% of URLs cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot ranked in Google's top 10 for the same prompt. Perplexity was the outlier, with nearly one in three citations pointing to top-10 pages (Ahrefs).

Google's official position is that there are "no additional requirements" or "special optimizations" needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond normal Search best practices (Google Search Central). Both things are true: SEO is the entry ticket for Google's AI features, but it doesn't decide most of what ChatGPT and other assistants cite. That gap is what AEO covers.

Where do AEO and SEO overlap?

A lot of the work counts twice:

  • Clear, server-rendered pages that crawlers can read.
  • Answer-first writing: a direct answer up top, question-style headings, tables and short definitions.
  • Comparison, alternatives and pricing pages that match how buyers search and ask.
  • Author names, dates and credentials that make a page trustworthy.
  • Earned links and mentions from respected publications.

The difference is emphasis. SEO treats a mention on another site mainly as a backlink. AEO treats it as the answer itself, because AI engines build "best X for Y" answers largely from roundups, review sites, Reddit threads and press coverage. That's why PR matters so much more for AEO than it ever did for SEO.

How do you measure AEO differently from SEO?

SEO has Search Console. AEO has no equivalent from the engines, so you build your own panel:

  1. Mention rate: the share of your buyer questions where you're named.
  2. Citation rate: how often your own pages are linked as a source.
  3. Share of voice: your mentions compared with competitors'.
  4. Accuracy: whether the engine describes your category, pricing and ICP correctly.
  5. AI referral traffic: visits from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and similar in your analytics.

Track each engine separately. Similarweb's data shows ChatGPT's share of generative AI web traffic fell from about 76% in June 2025 to about 53% by May 2026 as Gemini and Claude grew (Similarweb, July 2026), so a ChatGPT-only check misses a growing slice of buyers. Also expect referral patterns to shift with product updates: Similarweb found that after a 7 May 2026 ChatGPT change to how brand links appear, about 60% of ChatGPT referrals began landing on homepages, up from roughly 26–32% before (Similarweb). Make sure your homepage converts.

What doesn't change?

Good marketing still wins. Know your buyer's questions, answer them better than anyone, and get credible people to talk about you. SEO, AEO and GEO are three views of the same job: being the obvious answer when a buyer goes looking.

Do you need an AEO agency, an SEO agency, or both?

If your SEO is weak, fix that first; AI Overviews draw on Google's index. If your SEO is fine but AI answers name your competitors, you need someone who can earn third-party mentions, which is closer to PR than to technical SEO. FAQ

What is the difference between AEO and SEO?

SEO gets your pages ranked in search results so buyers click through. AEO gets your brand named and cited inside AI-generated answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. SEO is the foundation; AEO adds quotable pages and mentions on the third-party sources AI engines cite.

Is GEO the same as AEO?

In practice, yes. Both describe earning visibility in AI-generated answers. GEO was formalised in a 2024 academic paper, and some practitioners use it to stress how AI represents your brand, but most marketers use the two terms interchangeably.

Is SEO dead because of AI search?

No. Google says appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode needs no special optimisation beyond normal Search best practices, so SEO remains the entry ticket for Google's AI features. What's changed is that ranking alone earns fewer clicks and doesn't decide most of what ChatGPT cites.

Does ranking on Google get you cited by ChatGPT?

Not reliably. Ahrefs found only about 12% of URLs cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot ranked in Google's top 10 for the same prompt. Perplexity's citations overlap with Google's top 10 more often, at nearly one in three.

Which should a startup do first, AEO or SEO?

Fix basic SEO hygiene first (crawlable, server-rendered, answer-first pages), then put most effort into AEO on narrow buyer questions you can realistically win. A new domain is unlikely to outrank incumbents for broad terms quickly, but it can earn citations on specific prompts.

How do you measure AEO?

Run a fixed set of unbranded buyer questions on each engine, several times each, and track mention rate, citation rate, share of voice and accuracy. Add AI referral traffic from your analytics and a "How did you hear about us?" field on demo forms.
