Metamorphosis'26 Recap: What Happened at India's Web3 x AI Summit in Hyderabad

Recap of Metamorphosis'26, India's Web3 x AI summit at T-Hub, Hyderabad (26–27 Sep 2026): speakers, partners, themes and lessons on growing a tech event.

By Anupam Varshney, Founder, Octaloop

Metamorphosis'26 was the fifth edition of Metamorphosis, a Web3 x AI infrastructure summit run by Octaloop as the flagship event of the India Blockchain & AI Tour (IBT). It took place on 26–27 September 2026 in Hyderabad. A speaker, sponsor and VIP reception was held at Rumor Sky Lounge on 26 September, and the main event ran at The Marvel, T-Hub, on 27 September. It brought founders, enterprise AI leaders, Telangana government officials, investors and developers together for keynotes, panels, a workshop and a startup Demo Day.

It was also the first time Metamorphosis came to Hyderabad. The earlier four editions ran in Bengaluru and Delhi.

Top moments

Enterprise AI from IBM. Radhakrishna Bollineni, IBM's VP of Artificial Intelligence, opened the morning's enterprise track with a session on AI working at real scale. It set the tone for a day that was mostly about AI being deployed, not just demoed.

Kshitij Shrivastava's Sandisk keynote: "Imagination to Impact: AI Makes Everyone a Builder. What Makes You a Winner?" The keynote argued that AI tools have made almost anyone able to build, so the edge now comes from what you choose to build and how you ship it.

Bitcoin in India, from boardroom to retail. Bipin Chhaganbhai Patel (Merkle Value) and Balachandra Pulavarthi (ZebPay) joined a panel on "Bitcoin Adoption in India: Institutional Pathways vs Retail Reality". Bipin returned for a fireside chat with Anupam Varshney later in the day.

The Government of Telangana was also on stage. Meraj Faheem (Telangana Innovation Cell) and Sarvesh Singh (Lifesciences & Pharma) spoke on how the state backs innovation and on Hyderabad's life sciences hub.

More from the day

  • Startup Demo Day. Local Web3 and AI startups pitched live to VCs.
  • Hands-on sessions. India Crypto Research ran a one-hour workshop, and C'ROC presented on AI in blockchain security.
  • Partner booths from Merkle Value, Mudrex and India Crypto Research were open all day. Mudrex posted live from the venue on 27 September.
  • Speakers from industry. Talks from Criterion AI, Botella AI Labs, Quirq, 1Works.ai and OTIS covered AI agents, developer tooling and AI in the elevator industry.

Key themes and takeaways

These themes come from the session topics on the agenda.

  1. AI is the headline and Web3 is the infrastructure. Most sessions were about AI: enterprise AI, AI agents, developer tools and AI in industry. Web3 came up as the layer for identity, security and payments rather than as a topic on its own.
  2. AI agents need identity and trust. Talks on autonomous agents on-chain and decentralised identity asked how AI agents can act without someone having to trust them blindly.
  3. Security is moving to AI. C'ROC's session covered using AI to trace and respond to on-chain attacks.
  4. The state is an active participant. Telangana's innovation and life-sciences leaders came to talk about putting technology into use, not just policy.
  5. India from AI consumer to AI creator. A recurring idea from the builder talks was that Indian engineers should build AI, not just use it.

What we learned growing a tech event (and what it means for organisers)

Metamorphosis has moved from Bengaluru to Delhi to Hyderabad over five editions. These are the lessons we'd pass on to any founder or conference organiser trying to grow a tech event.

1. Partner with the local ecosystem hub, not just a venue. Booking T-Hub gave us more than a hall. T-Hub shared the event with its startup community and put it on its events calendar. Pick a venue that brings its own audience with it.

2. Book the room only once your positioning is clear. T-Hub approved the event because its AI, Web3 and startup focus fit the venue's mission. A clear one-line answer to "who is this for and what do they leave with?" opens doors with venues, speakers and sponsors.

3. Mix government, enterprise and builders. A crypto-only lineup limits who will come. Putting state innovation leaders, IBM and PwC alongside founders and engineers widened the audience and the press angles.

4. Give every ticket tier a clear outcome. Explorer gave access, VIP gave networking and the after-party, Startup gave a Demo Day pitch slot, and Elite gave matchmaking and hosting. When each tier has a clear outcome, upgrades are easy to sell.

5. Programme around participation. Workshops, Demo Day and a giveaway at the close keep people in the room until the last session. That matters to sponsors with booths.

6. Plan for things outside your control. Weather, illness and market cycles can all affect an event. Have a clear refund and transfer policy ready before you need it.

7. Treat the event as a content engine. Every speaker bio, talk title and recap page is something AI search engines can cite.
